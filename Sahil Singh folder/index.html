My journey

The exam I never got to write.

Just before my NEET exam in 2025, I met with a lethal accident. I survived — but my lower body took the real hit. My femur fractured, and the ligaments and tendons in my knee ruptured. My first operation happened on 22 February 2025. The doctors held off on the knee surgery for four months, hoping the ligaments would heal on their own. They didn't.

On 25 April 2025, I was given the date for my second operation. By then, my NEET exam was already over. I never got to attempt it. I spent the next month preparing myself, physically and mentally, for another surgery — and on 24 June 2025, my second operation happened. The four months after that went into medicines, painful physiotherapy, and recovery.

By November, free of hospital appointments, I started my preparation again — from scratch, nine months behind everyone else. It felt impossible. While others were finishing their syllabus, I was starting mine. My legs would swell up if I sat too long, fever would creep in, and standing was its own battle some days. But I never gave up. Every single day, I pushed as far as my body would let me — and in Re-NEET 2026, one of the toughest papers in recent history, I scored 490.

I'm not satisfied. I refuse to go into BDS or AYUSH carrying the guilt that my injury stopped me from performing better. So with everything I have, I've taken my final drop — and I'm working day and night to make this dream come true next year, no matter what it takes.

The goal

One more year. One real shot.

Somewhere in the middle of all this — the operations, the physio, the nine-month gap — the goal never changed. I want to be a doctor. Not just any seat through BDS or AYUSH, but a real MBBS seat, and eventually, a career in cardiology.

NEET 2026 proved to me that I could come back from zero and still score 490 on one of the toughest papers ever set. NEET 2027 is where I prove I can do better — with a body that's finally healed and a mind that never stopped.
